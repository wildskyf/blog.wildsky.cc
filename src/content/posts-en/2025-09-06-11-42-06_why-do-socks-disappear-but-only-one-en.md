While reading Taleb's "The Black Swan" recently, I suddenly thought this example would be perfect for explaining **Silent Evidence**. I'd like to share this concept and some possible solutions I came up with for this problem.

## Silent Evidence: The Evidence We Cannot See

When we complain about "losing another sock," we're actually only observing one situation: "the lonely single sock left behind." This lonely sock acts like a witness, reminding us that a sock has gone missing.

But what if both socks disappeared? In this case, we would <mark>never realize any socks were lost</mark>.

No witness, no reminder—it's as if nothing happened at all. Our brains wouldn't even register this event.

## The Solution?

The crux lies in "our socks come in pairs," so perhaps if we break this rule, we won't notice any sock losses?

(Friendly reminder: "not noticing" only provides peace of mind—you'll still suffer the actual loss XD)

I propose two solutions:

### Solution 1: Always wear two socks that don't form a pair

This approach has an additional benefit: choosing socks becomes super fast!

Every morning, just randomly pick two different socks and you're ready to go. The only thing you might have to endure is others' stares, but isn't it a great trade-off—lose one thing, gain two?

### Solution 2: Buy all socks in the same color

This is a refined version of the above approach. You don't have to endure strange looks while still enjoying the speed benefit.

Most importantly, it solves the problem: we won't discover after doing laundry that we're missing another sock!

Unless... you count the total. Once you find the total number of socks has become odd, you'll know you've lost one again.

## Conclusion

I hope after reading this article, you now know how to avoid **discovering** that you've lost another sock. More importantly, through this everyday occurrence, we can understand how silent evidence affects our perception: we're always drawn to obvious anomalies while ignoring events that happen silently.
